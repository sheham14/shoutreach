⚡ Getting Started — First-Time Setup ▼
⚠ Do DNS first. Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC before sending a single email. Skipping this will get your domain blacklisted within days.

Step 1 — DNS Records

Log into your domain registrar (Namecheap, GoDaddy, Cloudflare, etc.) and add these three records:

SPF — tells receiving servers your domain is allowed to send email.

Type:  TXT
Name:  @ (or your subdomain)
Value: v=spf1 include:zoho.com ~all   ← replace with your provider's SPF string

DKIM — a cryptographic signature proving the email wasn't tampered with. Generated by your mail provider.

Type:  TXT
Name:  zoho._domainkey  (your provider tells you the name)
Value: v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBg...  (your provider gives you this)

DMARC — policy record that ties SPF + DKIM together.

Type:  TXT
Name:  _dmarc
Value: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com

Start with p=none (monitoring only). After 2 clean weeks, upgrade to p=quarantine.

Verify all three are green before sending:
SPF → mxtoolbox.com/spf.aspx  |  DKIM → mxtoolbox.com/dkim.aspx  |  DMARC → mxtoolbox.com/dmarc.aspx

Step 2 — Add Your Physical Address

Go to Settings → Global Sending Rules → Company Physical Address and enter your mailing address. This is legally required by CAN-SPAM (US) and CASL (Canada) — every commercial email must include a valid physical address. It appears in the footer of every email you send.

⚠ Sending without a physical address in your footer is a legal violation, not just a best practice.

Step 3 — Add a Sending Account

Go to Settings → Email Accounts → Add Account.

  • Zoho Mail: Host smtp.zoho.com, Port 587, your Zoho email + password
  • Gmail Workspace: Host smtp.gmail.com, Port 587 — use an App Password (not your Google password). Enable it at Google Account → Security → App Passwords. Requires 2FA to be on.

Click Test SMTP to confirm the connection works before proceeding.

Step 4 — Configure IMAP (reply detection)

Optional but strongly recommended — IMAP lets the system automatically stop sequences when someone replies.

  • Zoho: Host imap.zoho.com, same username + password
  • Gmail: Host imap.gmail.com, same App Password. Enable IMAP in Gmail: Settings → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP → Enable IMAP.
🌡 Domain Warmup Guide ▼
⚠ Never start at full volume. A brand-new domain sending 50 emails on day 1 will be flagged as spam immediately. Warmup is non-negotiable.

Gradually increase your daily send limit so that Gmail and other providers build a positive sending reputation for your domain.

TimelineDaily LimitNotes
Week 110–20Set campaign limit to 15. Send to your best contacts first.
Week 220–40Increase to 30 only if bounce rate stays below 2%
Week 340–60Push to 50 if still healthy
Week 460–80Increase to 70
Month 280–120Domain is established — can scale further
Month 3+120–200Full speed. Monitor reply + bounce rates weekly.

Pause immediately if:

  • Bounce rate exceeds 5% — the system does this automatically via the bounce circuit-breaker
  • Reply rate drops below 0.5% — this is a copy problem, not a volume problem
  • You receive spam complaints from recipients
Tip: You can adjust the bounce auto-pause threshold per campaign under Campaign → ⚙ Settings → Bounce Auto-Pause Threshold.
⚙ Building Your First Campaign ▼

1. Import Contacts

Go to Contacts → Import CSV. Supported columns:

email,first_name,last_name,company
drsmith@dentistry.com,John,Smith,Smith Family Dental
office@clinic.com,Sarah,Jones,Wellness Clinic

You can also paste raw emails (one per line) for a quick import — the system will extract what it can.

Contacts without emails (e.g. from the scraper finding only contact forms) are stored as prospects with form_only or no_email status. You can follow up with them manually.

2. Create a Campaign

Go to Campaigns → New Campaign and configure:

  • Daily Limit — follow the warmup schedule above (start at 15)
  • Send Window — hours (UTC) when emails go out. 9–17 = 9am to 5pm UTC. Convert to your local timezone.
  • Min/Max Delay — random seconds between sends. Default 45–120s. This makes the pattern look human.

You can edit all these settings later via Campaign → ⚙ Settings.

3. Add Sequence Steps

Each step is one email. Step 1 sends immediately on enrollment. Step 2+ wait the configured number of days after the previous step was sent.

Recommended 3-step sequence:

Step 1 — Day 0 (immediate)

Subject: Quick question about {{company}}

Hi {{first_name}},

I came across {{company}} and wanted to reach out about [your offer].

[2–3 sentences max. Keep it short.]

Worth a quick chat?

[Your name]

Step 2 — 3 days after Step 1

Subject: Re: Quick question about {{company}}

Hi {{first_name}},

Just wanted to follow up on my message from a few days ago.

[One sentence reiterating value.]

Do you have 15 minutes this week?

[Your name]

Step 3 — 7 days after Step 2

Subject: Last follow-up

Hi {{first_name}},

I don't want to be a bother — this will be my last message.

If you're ever interested in [offer], I'm happy to chat.

[Your name]

4. Assign Sending Accounts (optional)

In the campaign detail view, click ⚙ Assign next to "Sending accounts". Selected accounts will rotate round-robin. If none are assigned, the system uses global SMTP settings.

5. Enroll Contacts

Click + Enroll in the campaign view. Select contacts from your list. Each contact is immediately scheduled for Step 1.

To remove a contact from a campaign, use the ✕ button in the Enrolled Contacts table, or select multiple and click Remove Selected.

6. Activate

Click ▶ Activate. The scheduler checks every 60 seconds and sends due emails within your configured send window.

◈ A/B Variant Testing ▼

Each step can have multiple variants (A, B, C…) with different subject lines and bodies. The system assigns a variant to each contact once at enrollment and they receive that variant for every step in the sequence.

Setting up variants

  1. Open a campaign and click Edit on a step
  2. Click + Add Variant — add as many as you like
  3. Set a weight for each variant (e.g. A=50, B=50 for a 50/50 split). Weights don't need to sum to 100 — they're relative. A=70, B=30 gives a 70/30 split.
  4. Fill in the subject and body for each variant
  5. The base subject/body at the top is a fallback for contacts enrolled before variants were added

Viewing results

Open a campaign — the A/B Variant Breakdown panel (below the main stats) shows enrolled count, emails sent, replies, bounces, and reply rate per variant. Use this to identify which copy performs better, then retire the weaker variant.

Tip: Test one variable at a time — either the subject line or the body, not both. Otherwise you won't know what drove the difference.
{} Using Variables in Templates ▼

Variables let you personalise every email automatically. Write {{variable_name}} anywhere in a subject or body and the system replaces it with real data at send time. There are three layers — each can override the one below it.

1. Standard Contact Fields (always available)

These are populated from the contact record:

VariableValue
{{first_name}}Contact's first name
{{last_name}}Contact's last name
{{full_name}}First + last combined
{{company}}Company name
{{email}}Contact's email address

2. Contact-Level Custom Fields (from CSV import)

Any extra column in your CSV that isn't a standard field is stored as a custom field on that contact. You can then use it as a variable with the same column name.

Example CSV:

email,first_name,company,job_title,industry
drsmith@dental.com,John,Smith Dental,Owner,Dentistry

The columns job_title and industry are non-standard, so they get stored as custom fields. You can now use {{job_title}} and {{industry}} in your templates:

Hi {{first_name}},

I noticed you're the {{job_title}} at {{company}} — I work with a lot of {{industry}} practices...

Each row can have different values (John might have job_title=Owner while Sarah has job_title=Practice Manager). Each contact's email is personalised with their own data.

3. Campaign-Level Variables (same value for all contacts in a campaign)

These are defined per-campaign in Campaign → ⚙ Settings → Campaign Variables. They apply to every contact in that campaign and are useful for things that are consistent across a batch — like the city you're targeting, a niche-specific term, or a personalised offer.

Example use case: You're running a campaign targeting dentists in Brampton.

In Campaign Settings, add a variable:

Key:   location
Value: Brampton

Then use it in your template:

Hi {{first_name}},

I came across {{company}} while looking at dental practices in {{location}}...

Every contact in this campaign will get "Brampton" for {{location}}. If you run a separate campaign for Mississauga, set location = Mississauga there — same template, different campaign.

Priority order (highest wins): Standard contact fields > Contact custom fields > Campaign variables.
This means if a contact has a custom field named location, it overrides the campaign-level location for that specific contact only.

Leaving a variable unreplaced

If a {{variable}} has no matching value (typo in the name, missing from contact data, not defined in campaign), it stays as-is in the email — the literal text {{variable}} will appear. Always preview your emails before activating to catch this.

✉ Email Copy Tips ▼

Available variables

Use these in any subject or body field — they're replaced with the contact's actual data at send time. See the Using Variables in Templates section above for the full guide including custom fields and campaign variables.

VariableReplaced with
{{first_name}}Contact's first name
{{last_name}}Contact's last name
{{full_name}}First + last name combined
{{company}}Company name
{{email}}Contact's email address
{{any_csv_column}}Custom field from CSV import (e.g. {{job_title}})
{{your_key}}Campaign variable defined in Campaign → ⚙ Settings

Do

  • Keep it short — under 150 words per email. Busy people skim.
  • Personalise with {{first_name}} and {{company}}
  • Ask one clear question at the end
  • Write in plain text style — no logos, no images, no heavy HTML
  • Use a specific, honest subject line
  • Make the follow-ups feel like genuine thread replies (Re: prefix, brief, reference previous message)

Don't

  • Use spam trigger words: "free", "guaranteed", "limited time offer", "click here", "earn money"
  • Add images, banners, or company logos — they scream bulk email
  • Send to catch-all or role addresses: info@, contact@, admin@, support@
  • Include more than one link per email
  • Use HTML formatting (bold, colours, bullet lists) — it hurts deliverability in cold outreach
  • Send more than 3 follow-ups to the same contact — it damages your domain reputation
Tip: If your reply rate drops below 0.5%, it's a copy problem — pause and rewrite your sequence before increasing volume.

◧ Troubleshooting ▼

Emails going to spam / Promotions

  1. Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are all green at mxtoolbox.com — all three must pass
  2. Check your email copy for spam trigger words (free, guaranteed, click here)
  3. Reduce your daily limit — you may be sending too fast for your domain's age
  4. Avoid HTML formatting in cold emails — plain-text style performs better
  5. Don't include images or more than one link

SMTP connection fails

  1. For Gmail: ensure you're using an App Password, not your Google account password. 2FA must be enabled first.
  2. For Gmail: confirm SMTP is enabled — Gmail Settings → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP
  3. Try port 465 with SSL if port 587 with TLS fails
  4. For Zoho: use your Zoho account password (not an app password unless you've specifically generated one)

Scheduler isn't sending

  • Check the status dot in the bottom-left sidebar — it should show green. If offline, refresh the page (the scheduler starts automatically with the Flask app).
  • Check the Activity Log for error messages from the scheduler
  • Confirm the campaign is set to Active (not Paused)
  • Confirm the current time is within your campaign's send window (UTC)
  • Check that enrolled contacts have status queued and a next_send_at in the past

Reply detection not working

  • Confirm IMAP is configured and the Test IMAP button passes in Settings → Email Accounts
  • For Gmail: IMAP must be explicitly enabled in Gmail settings
  • The reply check runs every 15 minutes — it won't be instant

Campaign auto-paused

The bounce circuit-breaker paused it. Check the Activity Log for the exact bounce rate. Clean your contact list (remove invalid addresses), then re-activate the campaign and consider lowering your daily limit temporarily.
